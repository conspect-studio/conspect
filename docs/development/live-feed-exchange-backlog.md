# Live Feed Exchange

Planned · [Implementation issue #205](https://github.com/conspect-studio/conspect/issues/205)

An operator on location can offer live camera feeds to other Conspect newsrooms. The contributor chooses which feeds to offer and sets a per-minute rate for each clean or branded version, including zero. A receiving newsroom (the taker) can find an offer by story location, select **Book feed** and use the delivered feed as an ordinary source. **Release feed** ends the booking. **Take** remains the switcher action that puts a source to air.

## Engine and console backlog

| Item | Deliverable and acceptance |
| --- | --- |
| LFX-1 · Offer contribution feeds | An authorised contributor enables exchange publishing explicitly, creates a story with a chosen map location and offers individual named feeds such as Wide and Tight. Each feed has its own availability. The contributor can offer either or both versions and set a non-negative rate for each. Offer Clean only when an unbranded signal is available. Expose published versions and their current signal status in the console. Withdrawing one feed leaves the others available. |
| LFX-2 · Deliver the booked version | Bind a published feed to its selected camera and associated audio. Produce the clean version without the contributor's channel branding and the branded version with the agreed channel branding applied before delivery. Bind the delivery credential to one booking, receiving organisation, feed and version. A taker entitled to branded receives only that rendition. A failure to render the required branding suspends the affected delivery. Reuse routing and encode-once fan-out where the source, branding and encoding settings match. |
| LFX-3 · Receive and release | Show available stories on a map in the console, with feeds, available versions, contributor, signal status and quoted per-minute rates supplied by the exchange service. Selecting **Book feed** stages an independently routable source on the taker's engine. Availability checks and failed connection attempts incur no usage. Successful live receipt starts metering even with the source only in preview. Tally, recording, layout changes and taking a source to or from air do not change the booking. |
| LFX-4 · Account for delivered time | Report authenticated, sequenced delivery intervals against the stable booking ID to the web service. Reconcile repeated, delayed and out-of-order reports once. Pause metering during confirmed loss of delivery, including while displaying a held frame or slate. A supervised reconnect may resume the same valid booking. Release, withdrawal, insufficient funded time or an expired authorisation stops delivery and ends billable time. Use bounded, expiring delivery authorisations so a disconnected client cannot continue an unbounded booking. |
| LFX-5 · Protect program output | Keep exchange control, authorisation renewal, usage reporting and delivery consumers off the output-clock thread. Exercise a stalled taker, disconnected relay and unavailable exchange service. Local program output keeps its cadence and an unrelated feed or booking continues normally. Keep ingest credentials, engine endpoints and exact camera positions out of catalogue responses. |

Conspect web owns the organisation catalogue, story map data, rate quotes, booking and authorisation service, prepaid balance, contributor earnings and usage reconciliation. The engine supplies media and evidence of delivery. Use a common booking ID across both platforms. Determine transport, relay topology, the test for usable media and timeout/grace rules during joint implementation design. Keep the delivery intervals independent of billing increments. The engine must never debit a wallet from local tally or wall-clock session age.

## Proposed Conspect-to-Conspect media path

Pilot Cloudflare's managed MoQ relays for native engine-to-engine contribution and distribution. Keep MPEG-TS over encrypted SRT as an interoperability path for outside encoders or receivers. Reuse the transport adapter, authorised subscriptions and bounded queues in [#204](https://github.com/conspect-studio/conspect/issues/204). Native receipt, booking enforcement and WAN rate allocation are additional exchange work.

Jane's engine publishes one contribution master per active camera when she permits a trusted origin to produce the branded and lower-rate renditions. The origin shares one encode of each required rendition across takers. A branded-only camera can burn in the mark on site. Account for every rendition that actually crosses the field link. Cloudflare forwards media objects; the producer or a trusted processor creates the renditions.

**Paid-service gate.** Pin a compatible transport draft and media mapping and decode actual HEVC and audio on a second Conspect engine. Validate late join at a random-access point, A/V sync, recovery and subscriber isolation. Cloudflare currently documents an authenticated draft-16 endpoint, relay-wide publish/subscribe operations, up to ten token records per relay, and revocation that leaves existing connections open. Separate clean and branded entitlement domains. Prove a bound on how long usable paid pictures remain accessible after release, withdrawal or credit exhaustion. Candidate designs are epoch-based media encryption with booking-controlled key renewal, or an enforcing media gateway. A client-side disconnect or token revocation alone does not meet this gate. Verify relay pricing, quotas and service terms before paid deployment.

### Managed relay and endpoint roles

The direct pilot requires two running Conspect engines and Cloudflare's managed MoQ service. The contributor makes an outbound publish connection. Each taker makes an outbound subscription and decodes the live media into a local source. Cloudflare receives, relays and fans out the published objects. The exchange API on Workers handles booking and control; media packets travel on the MoQ path. A publicly reachable engine, a Conspect-operated ingest server and a per-booking proxy are optional in this design.

Start with the contributor producing the representations it can afford within its observed uplink and encoder budgets, including the agreed branded picture. A taker selects from advertised representations or supported decodable layers. A single high-rate HEVC track does not supply a low-rate alternative. Where a site can upload only one master and the service promises extra encodes or branding, assign that work to a media processor. Validate any proposed managed Cloudflare processing path against native MoQ, latency, rights and codec requirements before relying on it. Keep the earlier paid-service gate: for a relay-only pilot, prototype content encrypted at origin with separate rotating keys per offer/version and booking-scoped HTTPS key delivery. Demonstrate bounded loss of access to future usable pictures; an open transport can still consume egress after access ends.

### Continuous bandwidth and latency adaptation

- [ ] Start every session with unknown uplink and downlink capacity. Require no configured or speed-tested line rate, fixed per-camera allocation, or fixed bandwidth reserve. The operator chooses the quality/latency policy and editorial priorities. Capacity estimates and encoder targets are time-varying outputs. A quiet scene is application-limited: use bounded probes on a healthy path to discover newly available capacity.
- [ ] Run one sender control loop for all active sends sharing a physical WAN path. Observe acknowledged delivery rate, pacing or write backpressure, oldest queued media, RTT relative to its baseline, jitter and loss/recovery where available. Account for every representation sent, including audio and repair traffic. Adapt rates and headroom together: reduce encoder output when congestion persists, preserve priority audio and picture, reallocate spare capacity across cameras and increase quality again when sustained delivery permits. Retain transport congestion control.
- [ ] Each taker independently manages all subscriptions on its downlink. Combine arrival jitter, useful throughput and lateness with buffer occupancy, decoder backlog and decoded-frame age. Use bounded adaptive buffering for brief arrival variation, downshift for persistent pressure, and switch up again on recovery. Separate decoder overload, last-mile congestion and a weak contribution link. One slow taker must not degrade other takers.
- [ ] Keep end-to-end media age within the declared operational latency budget when the path is viable. Bound queues by time; shed stale material safely and resume at a decodable random-access point with the required configuration. During an outage report the feed as unavailable and return to the live edge on recovery. Maintain A/V sync. Measure capture-to-decode age using synchronised clocks or report clock uncertainty. Report the reason for adaptation and timestamp each measurement.
- [ ] Use hidden, time-varying impairment traces with two live HEVC cameras and two differently constrained takers. One fixture goes 80 → 50 → 80 Mb/s; only the network shaper knows those values. Also vary jitter at equal bandwidth, add competing traffic, switch a quiet scene to motion, slow one decoder and interrupt one link. Declare observable pass bounds for queue age, A/V skew, response/recovery time and late frames before running. Require quality to recover when conditions improve, stable media on the healthy taker and continuous program cadence. Record actual wire use and decoded output; there is no expected fixed Mb/s per stream.

## Two-camera ENG acceptance scenario

1. Jane is covering the BankABC incident in Cityville. She enables publishing, places one story pin and offers two live feeds, **Wide** and **Tight**. She sets a separate rate for each available feed/version combination, including a free **Wide · Branded** and a paid **Tight · Clean**.
2. A second organisation opens the pin and sees both feeds, versions, availability and prices. It books Wide/Branded and Tight/Clean. Two distinct source bindings appear. Test pictures and audio identify their original cameras, and the branding matches the version booked.
3. Keep both feeds in preview for three minutes of confirmed delivery. Wide/Branded has zero charge. Tight/Clean incurs three minutes at the accepted rate. Cutting between them makes no additional booking or charge. A failed initial connection produces no debit.
4. Release the paid feed and verify media access and accrual stop for that booking while Wide/Branded continues. Repeat with funding exhaustion, source loss and withdrawal. Duplicate notifications and reconnects must neither restart a released booking nor count the same delivered interval twice.
