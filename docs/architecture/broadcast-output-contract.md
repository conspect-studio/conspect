# Broadcast output contract — R3 decision for #202 and #203

**Status:** implementation design, not an implemented fix. **Base:** `08991820` (RC3 plus merged RC1 repairs). **Scope:** clocked encoded outputs of one program; guarded packet-copy passthrough in ADR-0030 is a different path. **Urgency:** #202 active SRT starvation and #203 requested HEVC delivered as H.264. Candidate `1d67eda4` (52-packet queue, cap 64) is held as a reproducer **only**: do not transplant it or describe it as a repair. No production deployment follows from this note.

## Decision and invariant boundary

A **program** owns an independent rational `OutputClock`, tick counter, overlay bake and audio sample clock. A process may share a monotonic time *reference* and decoded sources, but must not use one global master clock or one global baked canvas for all programs (ADR-0030 §2). One tick of a program produces a timestamped, immutable `Arc` baked NV12 canvas. A nonblocking, bounded offer of that canvas to **one independently admitted worker per distinct video rendition** follows. A slow encoder, another program, a client, disk I/O or a management lock never waits on the program clock or a healthy rendition. This is a deliberate change from the single bake/encode consumer: serial encoding of several renditions cannot be presumed cheap without measured worst-case mixed-codec evidence. Video workers encode once **per video rendition**, and fan their encoded packets to all bindings for that rendition; they do not each bake the frame or duplicate the audio bus. Audio mixing and sample-clock advancement occur once per program, off the clock thread, then one audio worker/encoder per distinct audio rendition/track produces shareable packets. Each worker has independent bounded admission, session/VRAM/throughput reservation and failure state. Saturation fails that rendition and its dependent outputs visibly; it does not silently switch codec, slow the clock, or shed individual video frames while pretending continuity. Shutdown and restart are per worker with finite drain/deadline; a blocked native encoder cannot retain unlimited detached threads or sessions: cap restarts/detach, quarantine the affected rendition, and report resource reservation as unavailable until actually released.

`VideoRenditionKey = (program_id, logical codec, profile/level, dimensions, rational cadence, pixel/color metadata, bit-rate/rate-control/VBV, GOP/closed-IDR policy, encoder options, placement/device)`; equal keys mean bit-identical coded video and may share one encoder. `AudioRenditionKey = (program_id, source bus or discrete track identity, codec, sample rate, channels/layout, bitrate, normalization/processing policy, sample-clock epoch)`. Track identity and epoch prevent accidental reuse after a track/routing change. Audio keys contain **no** video key, so one AAC program or discrete-track encoder is not copied merely because H.264 and HEVC video differ. `MuxBinding = (output_id, program_id, VideoRenditionKey, ordered AudioRenditionKey list, container/transport, time-base map, sink policy)`; each sink receives **only its bound video** and the selected audio streams exactly once in stable stream-index order. Reject an unsupported codec/container, track count or mux time-base mapping; raw-frame display and AES67 remain separate taps. WebRTC/WHEP and WHIP outputs bind the video rendition chosen by their actual negotiated codec: SDP offer/answer may select only an already admitted compatible key, or explicitly admit and open an additional rendition before accepting the peer. Return a codec-negotiation/capacity error if no key exists; never send the singleton program packets with a contradictory SDP codec. One peer's timeout/reconnect or renegotiation cannot change another peer's binding.

## Bounded packet window and interleave

Do not treat an `encode_frame_at()` call as one complete output tick: video and every AAC encoder can return **zero to many** packets, including delayed/reordered video and AAC on subsequent calls and at `finish()`. Packet envelopes carry program, rendition/track, original integer tick or sample position, encoder PTS/DTS, duration, keyframe/RAP status, byte length and codec-parameter generation. Preserve encoder reorder; never infer a missing packet from an empty call, and never restamp delayed packet DTS to the *current* tick. Stamp encoder input video PTS from absolute tick and audio PTS from its exact integer sample counter; rescale through checked rational arithmetic per stream. Require strictly legal mux DTS and bound encoder delay by declared encoder capabilities plus measured reorder; an encoder exceeding its admitted delay fails its rendition, not the whole program.

An off-clock **window assembler** merges all packets emitted for an encoded-time interval into an immutable `PacketBatch` (zero or more video and zero or more packets for **each** selected audio track). It closes an interval only after all active encoders' watermarks prove no earlier DTS can arrive, including delayed packets; the flush path drains every encoder and closes the final partial window before a finite trailer deadline. Window accounting includes *actual* packets, never a forecast of one packet per tick. Per-binding admission is an atomic transaction: check **entire** batch against byte, packet-count and oldest-packet/encoded-time-age budgets, then enqueue it whole or reject it whole. No video-before-audio partial burst, and no `try_send` per packet. A batch too large to fit even an empty queue is an explicit failure/recovery event, not a recursive split with hidden partial acceptance. All counters include queued plus in-flight ownership and shared `Arc` references according to actual retained bytes; define a process-wide cap on pooled canvas and packet memory too. A timestamp watermark/flush watchdog bounds reorder and unpaired audio: fail the affected rendition or binding when no safe window can close, never wait indefinitely for a missing video packet.

**Initial safety ceilings to implement and verify, not measured production performance claims:** at most 8 video keys and 32 audio keys per program; at most 512 encoded packets, 16 MiB of retained coded payload and 2 s of encoded media per sink queue; at most 256 packets, 12 MiB and 250 ms of encoded presentation span per batch; at most 2 GOP durations **and** 2 s of encoder/window reorder delay, whichever is smaller; at most 64 MiB of outstanding encoded payload per program (count unique allocations plus per-binding reference overhead), plus a separately calculated `Arc` canvas byte cap from dimensions × admitted in-flight ticks × workers. Every limit is independently enforced; an oversize IDR may exceed nominal bitrate by orders of magnitude. Validate an explicit maximum access-unit bound from encoder options/VBV and an empirical stress measurement **before admission**; reject unsupported/unbounded encoder configurations or IDRs larger than the fixed single-batch limit, even if the average bitrate fits. These values are conservative trial defaults; capacity/load measurements may tighten them and a documented config revision may change them, but never raise only packet depth to paper over #202. On cap/age failure, atomically mark that binding discontinuous, drop its queued undecodable GOP, retain no orphan audio, and rejoin at a new decodable IDR with contemporaneous audio at an exact sample boundary. Request/force an IDR under a bounded timeout; if impossible, fail the sink explicitly. A keyframe flag is not automatically a closed IDR: verify codec-specific decodability/parameter sets at each recovery seam.

Choose **libav interleave only** inside each mux sink, not a second unbounded application interleaver. Set and verify `max_interleave_delta=500000` microseconds (or a stricter measured value that still preserves full multi-track cadence) at mux header/open; do not assume this option is honored by all container/muxer paths. Require instrumentation of mux call input/output DTS, `write_packet` duration, maximum outstanding packet bytes, oldest interleave age and muxer option readback/behavior; force a 500 ms write deadline for interruptible network I/O and a 2 s total sink worker lifecycle/flush deadline as initial limits. If libav cannot demonstrate a bounded retained-byte and age ceiling for a particular muxer, that muxer is **not admitted** until an explicitly bounded owned interleaver replaces it; never run two unbounded layers. `max_interleave_delta` forces interleave flushing, not a monotonic-DTS repair. The application batch/window bounds queued ownership, while measured/enforced mux deadlines and byte/age caps cover what the library can retain internally. A slow HLS filesystem or file sink has its **own** finite worker and budget; on stalled disk/write it fails with explicit discontinuity/partial-artifact status (close or quarantine the artifact), not silent lossless spooling and not pacing shared encode, SRT or another sink. A failed HLS segment must not be advertised as decodable/complete.

## Config, resource admission, and status

The current `Pipeline::build` takes `config.outputs.iter().find_map(output_codec)` (`pipeline.rs:2590–2597`) and resolves one encoder for all outputs; `logical_codec` defaults unknown tokens to MPEG-2 and `resolve_encoder` substitutes MPEG-2 if unavailable (`pipeline.rs:383–428`). Remove both silent substitution paths for accepted outputs. Validate **the entire proposed effective configuration**, not only the edited output, while holding `AppState::lock_config_mutation()` and **before** `boot_model::commit_resource` or any durable acceptance. Apply this to POST/PUT/DELETE output, config import/replace, program/audio changes, startup/reload and all other paths that can alter binding or resource demand. Compute all program-specific video/audio keys and bindings, codec/container and SDP compatibility, exact rational cadences and integral sample-frame schedule, unique IDs/track mapping, GOP/segment relationship, counts, bytes/age bounds, NVENC sessions and placement/device reserve for the **whole** desired config. `can_encode` is a build-feature check only; also check actual linked FFmpeg encoder availability and resource reservations before accepting a concrete plan. No external I/O, hardware open or waiting on a worker occurs while holding this management lock: calculate a deterministic plan under it; commit it transactionally with the accepted config/version, then perform fallible live open off lock. Fail invalid config with typed field-specific 422 or 409 capacity conflict, preserve the prior accepted config, and never claim a successful live switch from mere validation. If runtime open fails despite prior capability check (driver/session race, mux open, network, device), retain desired config but publish **effective** last-good binding or explicit failed output with requested codec, actual codec if any, error and generation. No H.264/H.265/MPEG-2 fallback on a user-requested HEVC binding; operator must approve a changed request. Make-before-break is admitted only when both old and new reservation fit, otherwise use an explicit controlled-reset plan. Reject segment targets incompatible with GOP: segment boundaries need an IDR-led closed GOP at the selected cadence and an integer tick count; derive exact segment duration from rational ticks, or reject the request, never round a second independently from GOP. HLS segment metadata uses actual PTS duration and discontinuity on an interrupted segment.

Expose per-program clock ticks/late ticks, bake/watermark delay; per-key requested/actual codec, encode queue occupancy bytes/count/oldest age, emitted video frames/packets, exact PTS gap and encoder delay, keyframes, sessions and failures; per-audio-key sample count/AU count/gaps; per-binding admitted/rejected **batches and packets**, rejected bytes, queue and libav retained bytes/age, write time, peer state, keyframe-wait/reconnect/discontinuity generation; and desired versus effective plan/version. A healthy clock plus an empty/starved SRT viewer is an output failure, not success. Track per-peer counters separately from a shared rendition: a slow or reconnecting IPv4 or IPv6 peer must not affect another viewer. Recovery starts only with decoder-ready codec params + closed IDR and aligned independently clocked audio, keeps continuity/discontinuity signaling honest for SRT/TS and HLS, and does not infer lossless transport from successful enqueue.

## Release acceptance and empirical gates

* Run a continuous **60-second** real 30000/1001-fps program with H.264 HLS and HEVC NVENC SRT simultaneously, plus program AAC and **five distinct AAC tracks**. Collect actual video/frame decode and per-track audio AU/sample counts from each artifact/receiver, exact integer PTS/DTS and rational per-track cadence, GOP/segment IDRs and codec/extradata with `ffprobe` plus decoded-frame inspection. Compare against the tick counter and the integer 60-second expected tick set; accept no unsupported-codec success, no unexplained per-track gaps, no duplicated tracks and no hidden H.264 on the HEVC SRT binding. A header-only probe is insufficient. Inspect encoder-delay zero-packet calls and multi-packet/flush tail explicitly.
* Repeat with one deliberately slow SRT peer while another live SRT peer and HLS remain healthy; reconnect each peer, force large-IDR and encoder reorder/flush paths, exercise IPv4 and IPv6 on the dual-stack listener. Demand bounded byte/count/age occupancy, exact batch drop/discontinuity counters, decoder-ready recovery and finite stop. Stall a file/HLS writer and one rendition encoder independently; verify sibling cadence and full truthful failure status. Test closed-GOP versus HLS segment constraints and reject invalid capacity/codec edits before durable commit.
* Run a **10–30 minute** managed soak job only after the short gate succeeds; retain process/encoder memory, GPU session and load measurements, `ffprobe` tracks and tick/PTS audit for the whole interval. This is an actual managed job, **not** a scheduled task or a claim that a soak already passed. Coordinate the mda001 GPUs and start/end times with the parent ship-rescue operator/session before **any** host access; this design note authorizes no host action, restart or deployment. If sustained mixed NVENC cost, libav retained-byte/age behavior, max IDR size or decoder recovery fails the proposed ceilings, tighten admission or revise measured defaults and repeat the gate; never hide failure with queue-depth inflation.

**Remaining empirical blockers:** actual worst-case dual-codec worker cost and NVENC session headroom on mda001; validated maximum access-unit/reorder under the deployed FFmpeg/driver; verified libav interleave retention and per-muxer option behavior; real five-track mux cadence, SRT IPv4/IPv6 recovery, and sustained artifact integrity. The note settles the architecture, not those measurements or production readiness.
